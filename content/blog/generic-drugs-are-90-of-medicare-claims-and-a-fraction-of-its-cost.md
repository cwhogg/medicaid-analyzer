The title makes a claim the data can't support. Every row in this extract is labeled "Brand," and each one shows 100% of claims and 100% of cost. There is no generic-only row to compare against, so the "90% of claims" figure in the headline can't be verified from what we have. What we can verify is the brand-name side of the ledger, and that side tells a story about price rather than volume.

Between 2013 and 2023, brand-name claims grew from 1,188,033,578 to 1,393,568,104, an increase of about 17%. Total brand cost grew from $80,914,974,166 to $212,689,454,816, a rise of roughly 163%. Volume barely moved. Spending more than doubled.

## The Price per Prescription More Than Doubled

Brand-name cost per claim was **$68.11 in 2013**. By 2023 it was **$152.62**. That's a 124% increase in a decade, and the climb never paused.

The annual figures from 2020 forward: $116.73, $127.18, $137.95, $152.62. Each year added roughly $10 to $15 to the average claim. The 2023 step, from $137.95 to $152.62, was the largest single-year dollar increase in that stretch.

For a plan sponsor or a Part D beneficiary, the arithmetic is simple. A prescription that cost the program $68 a decade ago now costs about $153. Nobody is filling twice as many scripts. They're filling slightly more scripts at a much higher price each.

## Volume Was Nearly Flat for Years, Then Moved

From 2016 through 2021, brand claims stayed in a narrow band, between 1,288,529,871 and 1,296,301,995 at the high end. The count in 2021 (1,291,283,853) was essentially where it stood five years earlier.

Then it moved. Claims reached 1,327,726,106 in 2022 and 1,393,568,104 in 2023, a two-year gain of about 102 million claims, or roughly 8%. Most of the decade's growth in brand volume is packed into those final two years.

That matters because cost growth and claim growth were not previously moving together. For most of the decade, spending climbed while volume held steady. In 2022 and 2023, both rose at once. Total brand cost went from $183,158,961,883 in 2022 to **$212,689,454,816** in 2023, a one-year increase of about $29.5 billion, or 16%.

## What the Extract Leaves Out

The framing for this piece, that a small slice of prescriptions drives most of the bill, is a claim about shares. Shares require both sides of the comparison. This dataset gives us the brand numerator and no generic denominator, so the "fraction of cost" half of the title is an assertion we can't test here.

The brand figures are still informative on their own. A cost base of $212.7 billion across 1.39 billion claims works out to $152.62 a claim, and that average has risen every year in the series without exception. Brand cost per claim didn't merely grow faster than inflation in some years. It grew in all ten annual steps from 2013 to 2023.

The generic comparison would sharpen all of this. If generics make up the large majority of claims, as the headline asserts, then the per-claim gap between the two categories is the number that explains the whole structure of Part D spending. We have one side of that gap measured to the dollar. The other side isn't in the file.

So the verified story is narrower than the headline but not weaker: brand-name drugs cost Medicare Part D 163% more in total in 2023 than in 2013 while claim volume rose 17%, and the average price per prescription climbed 124%. How much of the program's total spending that represents depends on a generic figure this query never returned.
